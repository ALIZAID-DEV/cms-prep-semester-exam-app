Lecture 12: 📘 Definition of Computer Based Information System (CBIS)
A Computer Based Information System (CBIS) is a structured arrangement of people,
hardware, software, data, and procedures that work together to collect, process, store, and
distribute information for decision-making, coordination, control, analysis, and
visualization in an organization.
🔑 Key Components of CBIS
      Hardware: Physical devices like computers, servers, and networking equipment.
      Software: Programs and applications that process and manage data.
      Data: Raw facts that are processed into meaningful information.
      People: Users such as managers, IT staff, and employees who interact with the
       system.
      Procedures: Rules and methods that govern how data is collected, processed, and
       used.
🎯 Purpose of CBIS
      To support decision-making by providing timely and accurate information.
      To improve efficiency in organizational operations.
      To facilitate communication and coordination among departments.
      To store and secure information for future use.

In short, a CBIS transforms raw data into useful information that helps organizations like
the University of Sindh, Jamshoro manage academic, administrative, and research
activities effectively.
📊 Types of CBIS
      Transaction Processing System (TPS)
       Handles day-to-day business transactions such as fee collection, student
       registration, payroll, and library management. It ensures accuracy and efficiency
       in routine operations.
      Management Information System (MIS)
       Provides summarized reports to managers for decision-making. For example,
       generating reports on student performance, departmental budgets, or attendance
       statistics.
      Decision Support System (DSS)
       Helps in making non-routine decisions by analyzing large datasets and providing
       simulations or models. For instance, predicting student enrollment trends or
       evaluating academic policies.
      Executive Support System (ESS)
       Designed for top-level management to monitor organizational performance and
       strategic planning. It provides dashboards and visual reports for quick insights.
      Office Automation System (OAS)
       Supports office activities like document management, scheduling, communication
       (emails, video conferencing), and collaboration tools.
      Knowledge Work System (KWS)
       Assists professionals and researchers in creating and sharing knowledge.
       Examples include research databases, academic publishing tools, and design
       software.
