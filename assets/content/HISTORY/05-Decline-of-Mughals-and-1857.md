# Lecture 5 — Decline of the Mughal Empire and 1857

## Causes of decline after 1707

Mughal decline had several causes. Weak successors reduced central authority. Noble factions created political instability. Financial pressure increased because of long wars and administration costs. Regional powers such as the Marathas, Sikhs, and different Nawabs became stronger. Internal strife made it difficult for the centre to control distant provinces.

## Rise of the East India Company

The British East India Company gradually moved from trade to political power. The Battle of Plassey in 1757 is an important turning point because it increased British influence in Bengal and weakened remaining Mughal authority.

## End of the empire

The War of Independence in 1857 ended the remaining Mughal political structure. Bahadur Shah Zafar became a symbol of the uprising, and after 1857 British Crown rule replaced Company rule.

## Long-question structure

Start with the political causes, then explain economic and military causes, regional powers, British expansion, and finally the significance of 1857. Conclude that decline was gradual and multi-causal, not the result of one event only.
