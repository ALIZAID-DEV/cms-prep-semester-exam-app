## Accounting Overview

Accounting is the language of business. It is the process of recording, classifying, summarizing, and reporting a business’s financial transactions.

The father of accounting is commonly identified as **Luca Pacioli (1447–1517)**.

Important accounting topics include the journal or book of original entry, ledger, posting, footing, balancing, trial balance, adjusted entries, adjusted trial balance, final accounts, income statement, profit and loss statement, closing entries, balance sheet or statement of financial position, cash book, and petty cash book.

## Main Branches of Accounting

1. **Financial accounting** records and reports business financial transactions for external users such as investors and shareholders.
2. **Cost accounting** calculates and controls the cost of producing goods and services.
3. **Management accounting** provides financial information to managers for planning and decision-making.

## Business

A business is an organized activity in which people produce, buy, or sell goods or services to earn profit.

The main types of business are:

- **Trading:** buying and selling goods without changing them, such as a shopkeeper or retailer.
- **Manufacturing:** producing goods from raw materials, such as a factory making clothes, cars, or furniture.
- **Services:** providing services instead of physical goods, such as banks, hospitals, schools, and transport companies.

## ALCER Elements

ALCER represents five important accounting elements:

- **A — Assets:** what the business owns.
- **L — Liabilities:** what the business owes.
- **C — Capital:** the owner’s investment in the business.
- **E — Expenses:** costs incurred to run the business.
- **R — Revenue:** money earned by the business from its activities.

## Shareholders and Stakeholders

Shareholders are individuals or organizations that hold one or more shares in a company. Equity shareholders generally carry higher risk, have higher potential reward, and usually have voting rights. Preference shareholders generally have lower risk, a fixed dividend, and priority in dividend and repayment, but usually do not have voting rights.

Stakeholders are individuals or organizations with an active interest in the functioning and overall performance of a company. They may include employees, creditors, government, suppliers, and customers.

A **creditor** is owed money by the business and represents a liability. A **debtor** owes money to the business and represents an asset or receivable.

## Business Organizations

A sole ownership is a business owned and managed by one person. Its advantages include easy formation, full control, full profit for the owner, and relatively low expenses. Its disadvantages include unlimited liability, limited capital, and low growth potential.

A partnership is a business owned by two or more persons who agree to share profits and losses. Its advantages include more capital, shared responsibility, better skills, and shared risk. Its disadvantages include unlimited liability, profit sharing, partner conflicts, and slower decisions.

**Unlimited liability** means the owner’s personal assets may be used to pay business debts.

## Types of Assets

- **Current assets:** assets that can be converted into cash within one year, such as cash, inventory, and accounts receivable.
- **Long-term or fixed assets:** assets used in the business for more than one year, such as buildings, machinery, and furniture.
- **Tangible assets:** physical assets that can be seen and touched, such as computers and vehicles.
- **Intangible assets:** non-physical assets that have value, such as patents, trademarks, copyright, and goodwill.
- **Financial assets:** investments or financial claims that provide future income, such as shares and bonds.

## Types of Liabilities

- **Current liabilities:** debts that must be paid within one year, such as utility bills and accounts payable.
- **Non-current or long-term liabilities:** debts payable after more than one year, such as a long-term bank loan.
