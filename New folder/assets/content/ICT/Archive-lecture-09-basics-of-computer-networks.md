Lecture 9: Computer Networks
4.4 Basics of Computer Networks
Computer networks are like highways on which data travels. A computer network
connects parts of a distributed system, including hardware and software. It enables
sharing of data, devices, and resources—functions that are essential in today’s world.



4.4.1 Computer Network and Networking
(i) Computer Network
A computer network is a group of computers and related equipment connected by
communication links to share data and resources.
(ii) Networking
Networking is the act of joining computers and accessories to enable information
exchange and resource sharing.
    Plays a vital role in computers and telecommunications.
    Modern organizations rely on networking for fast, inexpensive, and reliable
      communication.



4.4.2 Types of Computer Networks
Computer networks can be categorized by size and purpose. The size is defined by the
geographical area they cover and the number of connected devices. Networks range from
a few devices in a single room to millions across the globe.
Types of Networks
      Local Area Network (LAN)

      Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)

      Wide Area Network (WAN)

(i) Local Area Network (LAN)
     Covers a limited area such as schools, laboratories, homes, or office buildings.
     Enables sharing of internet access, storage space, and printers.
     Built with inexpensive hardware like hubs, switches, network adapters, and
      cables.
     Facilitates sharing of data and software.


(ii) Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)
     Spans an entire city, college campus, or small region.
     Covers areas of several miles and may include multiple LANs.
     Provides fast communication but is expensive to establish.
     Typically used by large businesses or universities.
     Requires security measures to prevent unauthorized access.


(iii) Wide Area Network (WAN)
     Designed for long-distance data transmission.
     Connects cities, provinces, or countries.
     Uses satellites, microwaves, or telecommunication links.
     Commonly used by large businesses, research institutions, and educational
      organizations.
     May include multiple MANs and LANs.
     Requires expensive devices and dedicated connections.
