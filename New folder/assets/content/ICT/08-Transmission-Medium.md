Lecture 7: Transmission Medium
📡 Transmission Medium
Definition
      Transmission Medium (Communication Channel): Path (wireless or physical) between
       sender and receiver for data transfer.
      Data transmitted via electromagnetic/electrical signals through wires, atmosphere, or
       vacuum.


✨ Characteristics of Transmission
   1. Delivery – Data must reach the correct destination in the correct order.
   2. Accuracy – Data must be delivered without alteration.
   3. Timeliness – Data must arrive on time; late data is useless.


🛠 Types of Transmission Media
Broadly classified into:
      Guided Media (Wired/Bounded)
      Unguided Media (Wireless/Unbounded)

4.2.1 Guided Media
Signals transmitted via physical links (cables).
Types:
    1. Twisted Pair Cable
             o   Two insulated copper wires twisted together.
             o   Prevents external interference.
             o   Widely used in data/voice infrastructures.
             o   Types:
                     UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair):
                            Blocks some interference but vulnerable.
                            Used in telephones/less distant network PCs.
                            Cheap, easy to install.
                     STP (Shielded Twisted Pair):
                            Special coating to block interference.
                            Used in fast Ethernet, telephone voice/data channels.
   2. Coaxial Cable (Coax)
             o   Outer plastic covering with two parallel conductors.
             o   Used in cable TV, analog television networks.
   3. Fiber-Optic Cable
             o   Data transmitted as light signals.
             o   Core (glass/plastic) with cladding for reflection.
             o   Very high speed, large data volumes.
             o   Used in modern high-speed networks.

4.2.2 Unguided Media
Wireless transmission; no physical medium.
Types:
    1. Radio Waves
          o   Electromagnetic waves, omnidirectional.
          o   Easy to generate, penetrate buildings.
          o   Used in FM/AM radio, TV, cordless phones.
   2. Microwaves
          o   Line-of-sight transmission; antennas must align.
          o   Distance ∝ antenna height.
          o   Used in mobile communication towers, TV broadcast.
          o   Types:
                   Terrestrial: Antennas on Earth.
                   Satellite: One antenna in orbit, others on Earth.
   3. Infrared
          o   Uses infrared light (LED transmitters, photodiode receivers).
          o   Line-of-sight, short range.
          o   Cannot penetrate walls.
          o   Used in wireless LANs, short-range links.

4.2.3 Transmission Impairments
Defects during data transmission.
Causes:
    1. Attenuation
          o   Loss of signal energy due to medium resistance.
          o   Increases with distance.
          o   Measured in decibels.
          o   Amplifiers used to restore strength.
   2. Distortion
          o   Change in signal shape.
          o   Composite signals’ components arrive at different times due to varying speeds.
          o   Causes phase differences.
   3. Noise
          o   Unwanted signals mixing with original data.
          o   Types: induced, crosstalk, thermal, impulse.
          o   Can damage signals.


🎯 Key Learning Outcomes (SLOs)
      Understand transmission medium.
      Recognize and describe guided vs. unguided media.
      Differentiate between them.
      Identify flaws/faults in transmission signals (attenuation, distortion, noise).
